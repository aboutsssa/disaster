Post-Disaster Socioeconomic and Environmental Monitoring

A synthetic-control and remote-sensing framework for reading recovery after major mountain disasters.

Introduction

Disaster recovery is not one story.

The earthquake shock is shared, but county trajectories diverge. Some places appear to catch up quickly; others remain below what similar, unaffected counties would have suggested.

Question How can we monitor social, economic, and environmental recovery after a disaster?

Analytical Structure

Three views, one recovery argument.

01 Ten county SCMs Each extremely affected county receives its own counterfactual path.
02 Extreme average unit Ten county-level treatment effects are averaged into one core recovery signal.
03 Intensity heterogeneity Extreme and severe counties are compared; general affected counties remain an extension.

Study Area

The 2008 Wenchuan earthquake reshaped a vast mountain reconstruction field.

On May 12, 2008, the earthquake struck near Yingxiu Town, Wenchuan County. With a surface-wave magnitude of 8.0 and a moment magnitude of 8.3, it damaged more than 100,000 km² and became the most destructive earthquake in China since 1949.

Method

Synthetic control asks a simple counterfactual question.

affected county weighted donors synthetic path post-2008 gap

For each extremely affected county, 42 unaffected counties are weighted to match the 2000-2007 pre-earthquake trajectory. The post-2008 difference is read as a recovery gap.

01 · Ten County SCMs

Start with local trajectories before averaging them away.

Every extremely affected county gets a separate SCM. This protects the local story: Anzhou, Wenchuan, Mianzhu, Beichuan, and Dujiangyan do not recover in the same way.

02 · Extreme Average SCM

The average unit summarizes a collective recovery deficit.

The average is not built by pretending ten counties are one place. It is built by estimating ten county SCMs first, then averaging their treatment effects into a single extreme-zone signal.

03 · Disaster Intensity Heterogeneity

Intensity changes the recovery comparison.

Extreme counties are the main treatment group; severe counties provide the comparison of stronger versus weaker disaster exposure. General affected counties are shown as an extension.

Story from 01 · Anzhou

Anzhou looks like a delayed catch-up story.

Pre-disaster fit is tight. The county falls below its counterfactual immediately after the earthquake, then gradually closes the gap and ends above the synthetic path.

Story from 02 · Wenchuan

Wenchuan becomes the emblem of visible reconstruction momentum.

The observed path rises above the synthetic counterfactual throughout the post-2008 period, suggesting a reconstruction-led growth trajectory.

Regional Extension · Trans-Himalaya

Wenchuan is one window into a wider mountain disaster system.

After the Wenchuan case, the same monitoring logic can travel along the Himalayan disaster belt: earthquakes, landslides, floods, glacier-lake risk, and uneven recovery.

2015 Nepal Gorkha earthquake Mountain settlements, heritage cities, and livelihood recovery.
2005 Kashmir earthquake Cross-border mountain vulnerability and reconstruction capacity.

Story from 03 · Mianzhu

Mianzhu shows the cost of being below the counterfactual.

After 2008, observed GDP per capita remains persistently lower than the synthetic path. The gap grows by 2020 rather than disappearing.

Discussion

The recovery map is heterogeneous.

Positive divergence Wenchuan and Mao County sit above their synthetic trajectories.
Persistent deficit Mianzhu, Shifang, Dujiangyan, and Beichuan remain below their counterfactual paths.
Interpret with care SCM clarifies divergence, but mechanisms need policy, investment, migration, and industrial evidence.

Conclusion

From disaster impact to re-inhabitability.

The Wenchuan case shows that recovery is not simply a return to trend. It is a spatially uneven reconstruction of livelihoods, population, infrastructure, and ecological conditions.

Measure the counterfactual. Tell the local story. Design for recovery.
